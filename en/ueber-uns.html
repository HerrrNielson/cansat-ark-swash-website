Mission & system

A CanSat collects data — and is designed to land under control.

Team SWASH is developing a satellite the size of a can for CanSat Austria. We combine the required measurements during descent with an experiment using a controlled autorotation rotor to steer towards a target. Our secondary mission includes both elements: a controlled rotor landing and NDVI analysis of plant images.

Primary mission

Measure temperature and air pressure during descent.

Our CanSat is designed to measure air temperature and pressure at least once per second and send the readings to a ground station via LoRa. There, we can analyse the measurements together with each flight phase.

An ESP32-S3 handles flight control. GPS, orientation and pressure sensors help record and interpret the flight data.

Inside the CanSat

One compact system with several tasks.

01

Flight computer & sensors

An ESP32-S3 reads temperature, pressure, orientation and GPS data, and logs the flight phases.

02

LoRa ground station

Telemetry is transmitted by radio to the ground station during flight.

03

Autorotation rotor

The rotor, swashplate and servos are part of our work towards a stable descent and a more precise landing.

04

Camera & NDVI

The Raspberry Pi CM0 is intended to capture images. NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) is a vegetation index that uses image data to indicate plant health.

Development status

We make our progress measurable.

In preliminary rotor tests, we are examining how mass, blade angle and airflow affect descent. Next, we will test a smaller blade angle and a larger rotor in controlled drops. We are also developing the NDVI image analysis; an additional ambient-light/UV sensor remains undecided.
