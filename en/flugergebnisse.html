Tests & flight

We share what we measure.

Here we document preliminary tests and will add our CanSat measurements after launch. The values below are from an initial rotor test and from simulations, not from a flight.

Current status

Initial rotor test by a car window

An early test with a 96.9 g test model and an 8° rotor-blade angle. The car’s speed provided the airflow.

Run Vehicle speed Measured force Angle to the horizontal
1 48 km/h 2.3 N 50°
2 70 km/h 3.5 N 30°
3 70 km/h 3.5 N 30°
Context: For a complete 300 g CanSat, we estimate a descent speed of about 19–22 m/s from this test. This is above the required 8–11 m/s and the 6 m/s target for a planned precision landing. Next, we will test a smaller rotor blade angle, a larger rotor and controlled drops. The force values have already been corrected for the spring scale’s 0.7 N zero offset.

Simulation

We fly on the computer before we fly.

Our flight software runs unchanged in a 3D simulation on a PC: rocket, ejection, gusty wind, GPS and a rotor whose three blades are each calculated separately. This lets us test 31 flight scenarios, including broken sensors.

What happens when something goes wrong?

Fault Before Now
Ejection 3 s late Rotor never opens, impact at 45 m/s Rotor spinning after 1.2 s, 8.9 m/s
Attitude sensor fails during spin-up Can hangs at 60°, up to 16 m/s Attitude corrected using GPS, 9.0 m/s
Rotor speed sensor dead 13.5 m/s detected from braking, 9.4 m/s
Pressure sensor freezes falls at 13.4 m/s detected, 8.7 m/s

Try the simulation yourself

Context: These are simulations, not measurements. Mass, centre of gravity, blade dimensions, friction and servos are assumed. Drop tests will check the model.
